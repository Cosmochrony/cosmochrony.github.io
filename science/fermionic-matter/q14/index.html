Toward Fermionic Matter from Projective Dirac Admissibility: Chirality and Electroweak Structure Rest on a Lorentzian Spin Solder and a Distinct Weak Factor

The spinor algebra is proved on supplied model data; the Lorentz reading rests on a spin solder [H-Spin] and the electroweak reading on a distinct weak factor [H-Weak], neither supplied.

Current Zenodo release: version 3.0 (2026-09-30). Official title: Toward Fermionic Matter from Projective Dirac Admissibility: Chirality and Electroweak Structure Rest on a Lorentzian Spin Solder and a Distinct Weak Factor.

Overview

This paper asks what the fermionic sector of the spectral approach rests on when fermions are sought in the Weil module of the admissible fibre on a supplied Heisenberg carrier, rather than added as external matter fields. The finite carrier acts through $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, which has no Lie algebra, so a real metaplectic model and a doublet carrying $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ are supplied model data. On them the algebra is proved.

The physical readings rest on two hypotheses that no source supplies. [H-Spin] solders the $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ acting on the doublet to the spin group of a four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric. [H-Weak] supplies a distinct weak factor: by Schur's lemma, a weak action commuting with Lorentz transformations cannot act on the same copy of the doublet. Both are missing identifications, not refutations.

Core contributions

  • Algebra (proved on supplied model data): $\mathfrak{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_\mathbb{C} \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$; on the supplied doublet the symmetric square is the adjoint and the exterior square the trivial line, and a Hermitian form selects the compact real form $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
  • Two readings, two hypotheses: under [H-Spin], the symmetric square of the spinor factor is a Lorentz sector and its exterior square carries no hypercharge; the $\operatorname{SU}(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ data come from the distinct $U(2)$ weak factor of [H-Weak].
  • Lorentz chirality: under [H-Spin] and the supplied Born–Infeld datum, the projected Dirac operator $\mathcal{D}_{\Pi,g,A}$ carries a canonical zero-order endomorphism $E_\Pi$, the spinorial lift of the parity is unique up to a phase and reverses chirality, and on an orientation branch taken as an input $E_\Pi$ is left-admissible; a non-zero left-admissible $E_\Pi \preceq 0$ breaks that parity.
  • Electroweak chiral selection: the $V-A$ structure of weak couplings is the representation content of [H-Weak]; left-admissibility is compatible with it but does not select it.
  • Hypercharge: under both hypotheses, the gauge-curvature $\gamma_5$-weighted $a_4$ terms impose anomaly constraints that select the hypercharge twists up to sign, rescaling and the exchange of $u_R$ and $d_R$, using the known classification; the $E_\Pi$-dependent traces are not analysed.
  • Three generations: the supplied selection rule $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ gives a gauge-singlet factor $\mathbb{C}^3_{\mathrm{gen}} \subset \ker(\operatorname{ad}_{\operatorname{SU}(2)} \oplus Y)$, conditional on a supplied rank-three carrier and on the two hypotheses.
  • Colour sector: a colour-coupled quark sector is written by tensoring with a supplied module $V_{\mathrm{color}}$; O31 version 2.0 withdraws the purported construction and group identification.
  • Generation splitting (model): on a distinct supplied generation doublet, a static $J_\Pi$-real, weight-preserving model operator has a degenerate outer pair; the step generator $\mathcal{G}_g = \log g$ of an ordered metaplectic step carries the exact $J_3$ component $\alpha = ts\,\theta/\sinh\theta$, $\cosh\theta = 1 + ts/2$, and no mixing component.

From the Weil module to a spinor algebra

Q14 identifies the admissible fibre with the Weil module $V_\rho$ on a supplied finite-Heisenberg carrier and fixed non-trivial central character; the axioms do not select that carrier. On the finite carrier the symmetries act through the finite group $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, while the metaplectic group of the real model acts on an infinite-dimensional representation with no two-dimensional piece. The passage to a doublet carrying $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is therefore a modelling step, and the doublet is supplied model data. The tensor identities on that doublet are proved.

Spinors and weak doublets are two different factors

The same algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the algebra of Lorentz spin and the complexified algebra of weak isospin, but one copy of the doublet cannot carry both. On an irreducible Weyl fibre, every map commuting with Lorentz transformations is a scalar (Schur's lemma), so a non-trivial weak action needs a second factor: the field carries a spinor index and a separate weak index, as in the Standard Model. Under [H-Spin], a compact real form of the Lorentz algebra is the rotation group of a chosen time-like direction, not an internal symmetry. Q14 therefore states the Lorentz reading as [H-Spin] and the electroweak reading as [H-Weak], and constructs neither factor's link to the fibre.

Spectral residue and chirality

Under [H-Spin], the endomorphism $E_\Pi$ entering the projected Dirac operator is the part of its square not resolved by the effective metric and gauge field: the spectral residue of non-injective projection. With the Born–Infeld parity datum supplied by O30's hypotheses, its spinorial lift is unique up to a phase and exchanges the two chiralities. On an orientation branch taken as an input, $E_\Pi$ has no right-handed self-coupling, and a non-zero such residue must break the parity.

That is a statement about Lorentz chirality. The chiral selection of the weak interaction, left-handed doublets and right-handed singlets, is the representation content of [H-Weak], and no argument in the paper derives it from left-admissibility.

Hypercharge from anomaly cancellation

Under both hypotheses, gauge invariance of the projected spectral functional imposes anomaly constraints through the gauge-curvature part of the $\gamma_5$-weighted $a_4$ coefficient. The hypercharge twists left free by [H-Weak] are then selected up to sign, rescaling and the exchange of $u_R$ and $d_R$, with a supplied three-dimensional colour module; the paper uses the known classification of one-generation solutions and does not reprove it. Terms of $a_4$ that depend on $E_\Pi$ are not analysed.

Three generations from $\sigma_c = 3$

Conditional on a supplied rank-three carrier, the selection rule $\sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ admits a spinorial reading as the multiplicity of a gauge-singlet subspace, giving the factor $\mathbb{C}^3_{\mathrm{gen}}$. O23 proves three-dimensionality only for the neutral sector of a supplied spinor carrier; carrier selection and its observable identification remain open, and no source reads this rule as three spatial directions.

A model of the generation splitting

Section 6 works on a supplied generation doublet distinct from the spinor factor, with the rank-three sector modelled as its symmetric square. A static $J_\Pi$-real, weight-preserving model operator cannot split the outer pair. The generator $\log g$ of an ordered metaplectic step carries an exact non-zero $J_3$ component, whose sign reverses with the order of the step, and no component along the mixing direction: no element of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ connects the two outer weights, so a complex metaplectic phase cannot produce mixing in this model.

Reading these operators as restrictions of $E_\Pi^2$ is not supplied, and neither is the identification of $\log g$ with an emergent ordering derivative: Q11 defines that derivative only on functions of the ordering parameter. The value $\varepsilon = 1/10$ is a matching value, and the amplitude is open.

Relation to the Cosmochrony program

Q14 follows the gauge–gravity synthesis of Q12–Q13, which organises gravity and Yang–Mills dynamics, conditionally, as Seeley–DeWitt responses of one admissible spectral functional. Its result is to locate what the fermionic sector needs beyond the admissible Weil fibre: a Lorentzian spin solder and a distinct weak factor. The geometric branch supplies a Lorentzian co-metric only conditionally, and Q11 supplies no Lorentzian structure to Q14.

Several consumer papers of the fermionic matter sub-programme were written before this version and are still to be aligned with it. Q14 does not derive the Heisenberg carrier, the colour module, the gauge group, the rank-three selection rule, or either of its two hypotheses from the admissibility axioms.
