Projective Information Loss and Fibre Erasure

A conditional information monotone; for the O12 capacity the label is absent from the observable, and no erasure is shown.

Read the preprint DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20466459

Overview

This article proves a conditional fibre-erasure theorem for the admissible coarse-graining hierarchy of the Cosmochrony spectral programme. The non-injective projection \(\Pi : \Omega \to \mathcal{O}\) defines a residual fibre-information functional \(I(c;\sigma(\ell))\), where \(c\) is a Weil-block fibre label and \(\sigma_c(\ell)\) is the BFS capacity profile at coarse-graining depth \(\ell\). Conditional on a structural sufficiency hypothesis [H-suff], the data-processing inequality implies that this functional is non-increasing in \(\ell\).

Version 2.0 applies the theorem exactly to the O12 capacity. O32 proves that this profile is invariant under scaling of the whole character block by any unit, so, under the sampling law of the campaigns, its distribution does not depend on the label. Hence \(I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0\) at every depth, [H-suff] holds for that observable, and the erasure bound reads \(0 \le 0\). This is independence in law, not equality of the profiles for each draw of the auxiliary characters. It shows that the label is absent from the observable; it demonstrates no erasure mechanism.

Version 2.0. The rank-level proposition is corrected (for the uniform O12 seed the rank is \(\min(2\ell+1,q)\), not the number of horizontal cosets), attributions to the withdrawn O31 results are removed, and the reading of the O32 colour-triplet campaign as support for [H-color] is withdrawn.

Core contributions

  • Conditional monotone. The functional \(I(c;\sigma(\ell))\) is non-increasing in \(\ell\) under [H-suff] and [H-sg].
  • Rank level. For the uniform seed of the O12 pipeline the BFS rank is \(\min(2\ell+1,q)\), the number of distinct second coordinates in the ball, independent of the label. [H-sg] is redundant at this level.
  • O12 capacity. Under the campaign sampling law the label is independent of the whole profile, which settles [O-1a\('\)] in law for general labels; the bound is \(0 \le 0\).
  • Numerical checks. Permutation-based Spearman tests for \(q \in \{61, 151, 211\}\) find no label dependence in step-by-step residuals, as the exact result requires. The leave-triplet-out \(\eta^2\) scores are unexplained measurements and carry no colour content.
  • Three axes. The coarse-graining axis \(\ell\), the large-\(q\) axis and the temporal cascade axis \(n\) (PTO) are kept distinct.

Strict disclaimer

This result is not a \(c\)-theorem. It does not count effective degrees of freedom, and for the O12 capacity it exhibits no depth at which information is lost, because none is present at depth zero. A non-trivial application requires an observable in which the label is present.

What the theorem does and does not establish

Establishes, conditional on [H-suff] and [H-sg]: a monotone information functional for the BFS coarse-graining hierarchy; the identification of \(I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0\) as the fibre-erasure equilibrium; and, for the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law, \(I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0\) at every depth.

Does not establish: a \(c\)-theorem; [H-suff] for other observables, seeds or sampling laws; the capacity-level composability [O-1b]; any non-trivial erasure or erasure mechanism for the O12 observable; any statement about colour.

Relation to the Cosmochrony programme

Fibre Erasure takes as input the ENI no-go theorem (which establishes \(S_\Pi > 0\) but does not order information loss across resolutions) and the BFS capacity profiles of the spectral admissibility programme. Together with the PTO paper, which handles the orthogonal temporal axis \(n\), it provides the information-theoretic frame of the admissibility cascade.
