From Admissibility to Quantum Structure: Phase Coherence and Correlations from Non-Injective Projection

Q1 establishes that phase coherence of a supplied complex Weil amplitude carrier is a structural consequence of projective admissibility, not a postulate. Theorem 2.7 proves that any admissible transition preserves the Born–Infeld indiscernibility of conjugate Weil blocks, and conditional on that carrier, the singlet correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ together with the Tsirelson bound follow from admissibility, O18, and O23 alone.

Overview

A central question in the Cosmochrony programme is whether quantum mechanical structure — phase coherence, Born rule, entanglement — can be derived from the projective admissibility constraints, or whether they must be added as independent postulates.

Q1 answers this question for the spin-1/2 sector, for a complex Weil amplitude carrier supplied by O18 and O23: phase coherence of that carrier is not assumed but proved. Theorem 2.7 establishes that any admissible transition on the Heisenberg graph preserves the Born–Infeld indiscernibility of the conjugate Weil blocks $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$, maintaining the metaplectic phase coherence of the fibre throughout the evolution. Q1 does not derive the carrier's own complex scalar structure — only its preservation.

Building on O18 (projective phase structure) and O23 (three isotropic neutral directions, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ structure), Q1 derives the bipartite singlet correlator and the Tsirelson bound, conditional on the supplied carrier, as structural consequences of admissibility's preservation of its coherence, with numerical validation for $q = 29$.

Central message. Given a supplied complex amplitude carrier, admissibility preserves its phase coherence, and that alone forces the quantum correlations — no further probability postulate is needed.

Core contributions

  • Theorem 2.7 — Phase coherence preservation: any admissible transition on the Heisenberg graph preserves the Born–Infeld indiscernibility of conjugate Weil blocks $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$, maintaining the metaplectic phase coherence of the fibre. Phase coherence is thus a structural consequence of admissibility, not an input.
  • Singlet correlator, conditional on the supplied carrier: combining Theorem 2.7 with the isotropic $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ structure of O23, the bipartite proto-state is identified as the spin-1/2 singlet, and the correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ is derived without invoking any further Hilbert-space postulate beyond that carrier.
  • Tsirelson bound, unconditional within the carrier: the bound $|E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) - E(\hat{a},\hat{b}') + E(\hat{a}',\hat{b}) + E(\hat{a}',\hat{b}')| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$ follows as a corollary of the projective structure acting on the supplied carrier, saturating the quantum maximum without a separate Born-rule postulate.
  • Numerical validation: all results are explicitly verified for the prime $q = 29$, confirming the analytical derivations in the concrete Weil representation setting.

Interpretation

The conventional approach to quantum mechanics introduces phase coherence and the Born rule as axioms. Q1 does not remove the need for a complex amplitude carrier — that carrier is supplied by the Weil representation of O18 and O23, not derived from admissibility. What Q1 shows is that, once the carrier is in place, its phase coherence is not an independent assumption: it is preserved by the non-injective projection constraints, and that preservation alone forces the quantum correlations that follow.

The key mechanism is the Born–Infeld indiscernibility of conjugate Weil blocks. Because $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ are structurally indistinguishable under any admissible operation, the fibre cannot select between them — and this underdetermination is precisely what preserves the metaplectic phase structure that underlies quantum superposition.

Admissibility governs which configurations belong to the fibre at all (support); it does not by itself supply a law relating the modulus of an admitted contribution to its admissibility weight, nor does it describe destructive cancellation between distinct admissible contributions to a shared observable event. Coherent non-detection may result from either mechanism, and Q1 establishes phase-coherence preservation only.

Physical implication. Bell-type correlations in this framework do not require non-locality or hidden variables: they arise from the projective structure of admissible transitions between Weil blocks, given the supplied carrier. The Tsirelson bound marks the maximal coherence compatible with admissibility, within that carrier.

Relation to the Cosmochrony programme

Q1 opens the Q-series thematic track devoted to the emergence of quantum structure. Its position in the dependency chain is central:

  • O18: establishes the projective phase structure on Heisenberg graphs from which phase coherence is inherited.
  • O23: establishes the three isotropic neutral directions and the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ structure that Q1 uses, together with O18, to identify the proto-state as a singlet.
  • Q2: extends the programme to spin-3/2 on the binary icosahedral group, using Q1 as its foundation.
  • Q3: completes the universal spin-$j$ extension for all admissible sectors of the binary icosahedral group.

Together, Q1–Q3 constitute the quantum sector of the spectral admissibility programme: conditional on the complex Weil amplitude carrier supplied by O18 and O23, phase coherence, the Born rule, and singlet correlators follow from projective structure without any further quantum postulate.
