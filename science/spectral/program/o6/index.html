Matrix-Level Dynamic Redundancy and a Conditional Upper Bound on the Cascade Exponent \(\beta\)

What one operator-level fingerprint supplies on LPS graphs: a span of dimension $q^2$ that saturates within the graph diameter, and only a conditional upper bound on the cascade exponent.

Read the preprint Back to Spectral Admissibility Programme

Overview

This article continues the spectral hierarchy programme by addressing the mechanism identified but not resolved in O5: matrix-level admissible redundancy as the possible origin of the small cascade exponent.

The key question is whether a sufficiently refined matrix-level encoding can produce a long pre-saturation regime in which the admissible frontier decays as a power law. O6 constructs the Steinberg-matrix fingerprint, the centred permutation matrix of a group element acting on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_q)$, and tests it on LPS graphs.

The result is negative for this fingerprint. Its span has dimension $q^2$ (the Steinberg module has dimension $q$), and along breadth-first balls the span grows strictly until it is full, then never grows again. On $X_{5,q}$ for $q \in \{29, 41, 61\}$, ranks certified by exact arithmetic reach $q^2$ at radius 4, 5 and 5: too few radii for any exponent fit. Version 2.0 replaces the floating-point table of version 1.2, which reported impossible ranks, and withdraws its readings of a saturation depth common to all $q$ and of a general no-go for fixed representations.

Core contributions

  • Matrix-level fingerprint: the Steinberg-matrix fingerprint $\mathrm{vec}(P_v - J/(q+1))$, in an ambient space of dimension $(q+1)^2$, whose span over the group is the endomorphism algebra of the $q$-dimensional Steinberg module, of dimension $q^2$.
  • Saturation without regrowth (proved): along breadth-first balls the span grows strictly until it is full and cannot grow again after a plateau, so the saturation radius is at most $q^2 - 1$ and at most the graph diameter, which is $O(\log q)$ on LPS graphs. Neither bound is uniform in $q$.
  • Certified numerics: on $X_{5,q}$, $q \in \{29, 41, 61\}$, full rank at radius 4, 5, 5 and first radius without novelty 5, 6, 6, checked by exact arithmetic modulo two primes. These are results for the three computed primes, not a law in $q$.
  • Conditional bound: under the unestablished effective growth hypothesis (GR) and a power-law Ansatz that this fingerprint does not satisfy, only $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}} \le 1/(1/2+\alpha)$ follows.
  • Withdrawn in version 2.0: the depth-6 saturation common to all $q$, the monotone decrease of $|S^*|/|G|$, the $O(q^2)$ and $O(1/q)$ bounds on the saturation cardinality, and the no-go for every fixed finite-dimensional representation.

Interpretation

The article delimits what one fixed-representation fingerprint supplies on one family of expanders. Its span is capped by a dimension count, and any breadth-first filtration of an LPS graph ends at the diameter, so the window it leaves is too short for exponent extraction.

  • Vertex-level encodings (O5) are capped at $O(q)$ directions.
  • The Steinberg-matrix fingerprint reaches $q^2$ directions within a few radii.
  • A longer window can only be sought in the explored cardinality, or on graphs with slower growth (O8, O9).

These results do not show that the cascade exponent, or the mass hierarchy, cannot arise from a fixed representation; that broader reading of version 1.2 is withdrawn.

Relation to the Cosmochrony program

O6 follows from the structural localisation achieved in O5. Spectral admissibility, capacity, rigidity, and stratigraphy fix the spectral backbone of the theory. O1 restores ordering through projective dynamics, O3 amplifies the hierarchy via valence growth, and O5 isolates matrix-level redundancy as the relevant mechanism.

The present paper tests the next level: an operator-level fingerprint of span dimension $q^2$. It finds that this fingerprint saturates too quickly as well, and records why: a dimension count and the graph diameter. It does not establish a general obstruction for fixed representations; the next stage of the programme examines larger fingerprints (O7, O8) and graphs with polynomial growth.
