Three-Step Path Fingerprints on LPS Graphs and the Capacity Growth Exponent

Why the first q-growing path fingerprint, whose span grows beyond that of O6, still fails to extract the capacity exponent on LPS graphs because exponential shell growth geometrically compresses the observable window.

Read the preprint Back to Spectral Admissibility Programme

Overview

This article continues the spectral admissibility programme after the reformulation developed in O7. While O6 found that its Steinberg-matrix fingerprint fills its $q^2$-dimensional span within a few BFS radii, O7 identified the correct observable as the discrete projective capacity $\Sigma_n$.

O8 performs the next step: it introduces the first explicit q-growing path fingerprint, namely a three-step permutation-path encoding in ambient dimension $O(q^3)$, and tests whether this is sufficient to produce a measurable pre-saturation regime for the capacity growth exponent $\delta$.

The answer is structurally sharp. The new fingerprint keeps generating directions beyond the $q^2$-dimensional span of the O6 fingerprint and opens the first pre-saturation window growing as $O(q^2)$ in vertex count. But on LPS Ramanujan graphs this window is compressed by exponential shell growth into only $O(\log q)$ BFS steps, which is too short to support a reliable exponent extraction.

Core contributions

  • Three-step permutation-path fingerprint: introduction of the fingerprint $\pi_3(\gamma)=\rho_{\mathrm{perm}}(v_1)\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{perm}}(v_2)\otimes \rho_{\mathrm{perm}}(v_3)$ for non-backtracking paths on $X_{p,q}$.
  • First q-growing admissible window: identification of an effective pre-saturation window of order $O(q^2)$ in vertex count, the first growing window in the O-series.
  • Rank growth over several BFS steps: the sketched span keeps growing up to $O(q^2)$ vertices; O6 2.0 proves no saturation radius independent of $q$ that would exclude this.
  • Geometric compression mechanism: proof that exponential shell growth on LPS graphs compresses the $O(q^2)$-vertex window into only $O(\log q)$ BFS steps.
  • Geometric obstruction: identification of a geometric reason, which also bounds the saturation radius of O6's fingerprint by the graph diameter, why the capacity exponent cannot be extracted on the LPS family at accessible sizes.

Interpretation

The article shows that a span larger than that of O6 is not enough: a growing admissible state space is not sufficient. Once the fingerprint dimension grows with $q$, the decisive question becomes whether the underlying graph geometry provides a long enough observable cascade.

  • The fixed fingerprints of O5 and O6 fail because they fill their span within a few BFS steps.
  • Three-step path fingerprints continue to generate new directions up to $O(q^2)$ vertices.
  • LPS shell growth is too rapid: the observable window in BFS depth remains only logarithmic.
  • The obstruction is geometric: the graph family itself prevents reliable exponent extraction, even though the fingerprint is structurally adequate.

In this picture, the unresolved problem is no longer merely to enlarge the fingerprint. It is to find a graph-growth regime in which a growing admissible state space unfolds over a sufficiently long cascade.

Relation to the Cosmochrony program

O8 follows directly from O7. Spectral admissibility, capacity, rigidity, and stratigraphy define the spectral backbone of the theory. O1 restores ordering through projective dynamics, O3 amplifies the hierarchy via valence growth, O4 shows that bounded flux and Cheeger expansion do not, by the argument examined, bound the cascade exponent, O5 localises the failure of vertex-level mechanisms, O6 finds that its Steinberg-matrix fingerprint fills its $q^2$-dimensional span within a few BFS radii, and O7 reformulates the relevant observable in terms of projective capacity.

The present paper tests a fingerprint of larger dimension. It shows that the remaining obstruction is geometric: on LPS expanders, exponential shell growth compresses the observable window before the asymptotic capacity-scaling regime can be resolved.
